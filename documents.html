Where the papers disagree

Where George drowned

The papers give six different answers on where George drowned. Mira'at (29 July 1930) says Long Island, a canoe on a lake. As-Sayeh (31 July) says Long Island, "the shore". Al-Hoda (29 July) names no place, only that neither could swim. Ash-Shams (1 September) says a lake in Rhode Island "near New York". Ash-Shams (1 October) says "one of the lakes of the United States". Al-Hoda (9 September) says a lake "about fifty miles from Brooklyn" (our translation, to be confirmed by a native reader). This is open. Long Island newspapers, the St. John's burial record and the county death record could help.

The time of George's service

The papers give two times for George's service. The Arabic newspaper Mira'at says two in the afternoon. The Al-Hoda notice of 28 July 1930, known only from a screenshot, says three. Al-Hoda of 29 July and 30 July prints "the second hour after noon", so three is the first printing's figure alone (our translation, to be confirmed by a native reader). We lean to two in the afternoon. This is open but small. The 28 July page itself is not in hand.

The day Nassib died

The papers do not agree on the day Nassib died. His death certificate says 2 October 1924, a Thursday. The three paid notices agree, printing "the evening of Thursday, the 2nd of October 1924" (our reading of the numeral, to be confirmed by a native reader). As-Sayeh of 6 October 1924 says he died "last Friday", which is 3 October. We lean strongly to Thursday 2 October; "last Friday" is probably a loose news-item error. A Brooklyn Eagle or Times Union notice of 3 or 4 October 1924 could help.

Where the papers disagree

The Awn children's ages

The ages of the Awn children do not agree from one record to the next. The 1920 manifest and the 1925 census give matching ages for three children: Joseph, Theresa and John. They differ for six: Aseen, George, Michael, Elias, Matilda and Anthony. Michael is 8 on the manifest (about 13 in 1925) but 16 on the census (as we read it). Elias is 6 on the manifest, but "Louis" (probably Elias) is 25 on the April 1937 Pilsudski cruise manifest; his petition says 3 May 1912, and the naturalization index says 1913. Matilda has three readings: "Matilde" is 29 on the manifest and 23 on the census, her petition gives 16 October 1899 (20 on 5 September 1920, 25 in 1925), and the naturalization index gives 1900. We do not know which is right; this is a question for the family.

Fred's birth and arrival, and Elias's entry date

Records give Fred Gorayeb, Matilda's husband, four birth dates. Matilda's petition says 17 December 1889 and arrival in 1902. His own petition says 2 October 1889 and 9 November 1903. His 1942 draft card says 5 November 1889. His 1921 passport application says 17 October 1890. Separately, Elias's papers give his entry at Rouses Point as 7 September 1920, but his brothers' and sisters' say 5 September. This is open, and Fred's own sworn papers come first.

Where the papers disagree

The date of Anthony and Helen's wedding

The papers give two dates for the wedding, one day apart. Newspapers of 3 January 1941 say "last Sunday", which is 29 December 1940. Anthony's 1942 petition says 29 December 1940 too. The Caravan of 9 January 1958 says the 17th anniversary was "December 28th". The 1966 obituary says the 25th anniversary came "last Fall", which does not fit. When Khaleel came to America

The 1915 census says 12 years, about 1903. His passport applications also say 1891: Havre about 21 or 31 October 1891 (in 1901), Havre about 22 October 1891 (in 1910) and Beirut about 10 September 1891 (in 1920). It is open, but leaning 1891.

The year Khaleel finished at the Beirut college

Where the papers disagree

When Murad came to America

The records give four different years for Murad's arrival. Harold's 1921 passport application says Murad left Syria in February 1890. The 1920 census reads 1886 as we read it, and the 1900 census says 1887. Ferris's 1919 consular registration says his father emigrated "on or about 1891". None of these is settled.

Tirkman's years in the United States

The records give two different counts of Tirkman's years in the United States. Her 1929 death certificate says 37 years, which gives an arrival about 1892. The 1929 death index also says 37. The 1925 census says 35 years. Which count is right is not settled.

Tirkman's family name

Tirkman's family name is spelled many ways: Bouinain, Bouman, "Boainam", Buainain and Abu Aynin. Ferris's 1942 death certificate reads "Bouainain", and the 1929 death-index entry reads "Bruinsin". The Social Security index lines for Murad and Tirkman, and Ferris's Social Security file, use "Bouainain" again. We do not know which spelling is right.

Tirkman's funeral day

The papers do not agree on the day of Tirkman's funeral. Al-Hoda and the death certificate give Tuesday 10 September (our translation, to be confirmed by a native reader). The Brooklyn Daily Times of the 10th says "to-morrow afternoon". This is not settled.

Ferris's age on the 1925 census

The 1925 census and Ferris's birth date do not give the same age. The census says he was 28. His 1942 death certificate says he was born 26 February 1896 (the FamilySearch death index agrees), which would make him 29. His age of 30 on his December 1926 marriage certificate fits that birth date. It is a one-year difference, and the household gave the census age. This is still open.

Ferris's birth date

Records give two different birth dates for Ferris. His 1942 death certificate and the FamilySearch death index say 26 February 1896. His Social Security file (a December 1936 application) says 2 February 1896. His own sworn application to the consulate at Funchal, Madeira, sworn 13 Oct 1919, says "February 2, 1896", so we lean to 2 February. Both dates fit his 1926 marriage age of 30, and his 1923 passport file (age 26 on 10 January 1923) fits both too. It stays open. His Cook County birth record or his own 1921 passport application would help.

Tirkman's age at death

The records give three ages for Tirkman at death. The 13 September 1929 obituary index says 61, born about 1868, but it was written by computer and the newspaper page has not been seen. The 1929 death index says 51, born about 1878. The 1925 census says 48 (about 1877). The index may have misread 51 as 61. This is a small conflict and still open. The 13 September 1929 page of the newspaper Home Talk the Star would settle it.

Murad in January 1920

The Oak Park census of 2 January 1920 lists "Saydah Murad", 48, jewelry, own store, as head of the household. Four days later, on 6 January 1920, "Sayyidah Murad", 48, born Syria, married, is an "Inmate" of the Chicago State Hospital for the Insane. In May 1910 a "Patient" named Murad Sayyidah, 39, born Syria, immigrated 1890, is at the Cook County Hospital for Insane. Either there are two men, one man was counted twice, or one entry is misplaced. This is not settled. The hospital's admission and discharge records may help.

The date of Ferris Saydah's wedding

The papers do not agree on the date of Ferris's wedding. The notice in the Arabic newspaper Mira'at of Thursday 9 December 1926 (the paper spells his name Fares) invites guests to the crowning "on Saturday evening" at 8:30, which works out to 11 December 1926 (our translation, to be confirmed by a native reader). The page image itself says "Saturday next". His marriage certificate says 18 December 1926 (the clerk stamped it 22 December). Either the wedding was put off a week or the printed notice slipped. Al-Hoda or As-Sayeh for 18-20 December 1926 may settle it.

Where Ferris Saydah died

The Mira'at al-Gharb item of 7 October 1942 says he died on the morning of the Saturday just past in Tenafly, New Jersey, where he lived with his family, and names no hospital (our translation, to be confirmed by a native reader). They agree on the day and on his living at Tenafly. We lean to the certificate, a signed official record with the attending physician. The newspaper wording may be loose.

Where Brooklyn probate files can and cannot be searched

Probate files are the court papers about a person's estate.
