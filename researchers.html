This page is for researchers and for relatives who want to check our work: the records we cite but could not picture, the dead ends, and what we are still searching for.

Records cited but not pictured

These are index and database pages, and newspaper pages, that we cite but do not picture on this site. Where a record holds only typed facts, the citation stands in for the picture. They are grouped by family line, and each entry gives its full citation. Anything marked “probably” fits but is not proven.

Dead Ends and Ruled-Out Records

Part of good research is knowing which records are not ours. These are written down so nobody has to search for them twice.

  • Joseph Owen, who died 27 April 1932 at 385 Clinton Street, is not Anthony's father. His death certificate (no. 9788) calls him a grocer of 55 whose wife was Sadie and whose parents were Michael and Mary, and says he had lived in the U.S. 18 years. Joseph (Anthony's father) died in September 1932, aged 62, married to Assin, with parents Peter and Teresa. The same cemetery (St. John's) and the same undertaker (Jere J. Cronin, Inc.) buried both, but Owen was a common spelling among Brooklyn Syrian families and nothing ties the two men together (a different man, since the ages, wives, parents and addresses differ).

  • A "John Khouri" fruit-store lead is not John Awn (Family, from Sarah).

  • A 1906 petition by a Salim Haddad (born 1883, in the U.S. since 1900, living on Washington Street in Manhattan) and a 1897 Kansas City card for a Salim Hadad are probably different men from Wassela's brother Salim, who was in Brooklyn by 1892 and in the June 1900 census.

  • The January 1898 ship card for ten-year-old "Wassilah Hadad" Probably describes a different girl, because the Hadads were already living in Brooklyn.

  • The 1920 Ishpeming census page for an Assad Kirkish household (pictured in Document 106) is probably a different Kirkish family. Assad's wife Sadie (born an Abraham, by her Social Security line) is probably not Antoon’s sister Sa’da; the Sada of the 1899 Negaunee birth return and the 1920 Munising census, wife of Abdalla Kirkish, probably is.

  • George Abraham Saydah of Bay Ridge is not shown to be related. His 1923 declaration says he was born on 15 October 1880 (his 1926 petition says 1882), sailed from Naples on the Prinzess Irene and reached New York on 29 or 30 August 1906 (last foreign residence Cairo), and lived on Ovington Avenue in Brooklyn, with wife Melia and three children born in Hoboken. His petition number is 68097. Nothing in the papers names Antoon, Khaleel or Michael, so he stays here until a record links him (a link is only a question for the family; FamilySearch, Brooklyn naturalizations, National Archives image group 007784999, images 703, 705 and 706 of 1018).

  • Antone Saddy, a 22-year-old Syrian lodger in the 1920 Manhattan census (sheet 10A, census district 1421; FamilySearch record 3:1:33SQ-GRNV-WRQ, image 1127 of 1176) is probably a different man, since Antoon Saydah was in Brooklyn with his wife and daughter in January 1920.

  • “Ambassador George Hakim,” Lebanon’s representative to the United Nations, on the front page of The Caravan, 22 June 1961, is probably not George Michael Hakim, the husband of Virginia. Virginia's husband George was a Brooklyn man, a Columbia graduate and an Army staff sergeant in 1945, and later a marketing executive in New Jersey. Nothing on the page mentions Brooklyn or the Saydah or Awn families, and Hakim is a common Lebanese name (Brooklyn Newsstand, Caravan, 22 June 1961, page 1).

  • A Social Security index line for “V Hakim,” born 1925 and died 2006, last living in New York, is not Virginia Saydah Hakim. Her obituary says she was born in 1921 and died on 26 August 2015, which is after that index line’s date and does not match it (Social Security Death Index; obituary in The Record, 27 August 2015).

Still Searching

A family history is never finished. These are the open questions, grouped by family line. Each says what is open and which record would settle it. If you know anything about any of them, please use the sharing form below.

The Aoun (Awn) family

  • The wedding of Anthony and Helen. Two Arabic papers and Anthony’s petition give Sunday 29 December 1940; a Caravan anniversary note of 1958 gives 28 December. We hold only an index line for the marriage license. The license itself, or the 1940 registers of St. Anselm’s or Our Lady of Lebanon, would settle the date and the church.

  • Anthony’s death. His obituary says he died suddenly on 6 July 1966; a memorial page gives 9 July, which may be the burial day. We do not have his death certificate or the cemetery’s burial record, and the Daily News notice of 8 July 1966 is known only from an index line.

  • Anthony’s country on paper. Records call him “Syrian”, “Syria” and, on his 1940 draft card, “France”. No record says why.

  • The house on 86th Street. Most records say number 95; one 1958 notice says 96. It is probably a misprint, but we have not shown it. The 1947 deed that is probably Anthony’s purchase of the house has not been requested.

  • The family in 1930. We have not found the Awn family’s 1930 census page.

  • Helen’s later addresses. An index of public records gives Helen addresses after her death in 2000. We treat index lines as pointers, and the certificate and memorial card agree on 26 January 2000.

  • Small age differences. Anthony’s age on the 1950 census (35, where his birth date gives 36) and Helen’s on the 1940 census (22, where hers gives 23) are each one year off.

  • Helen’s own records. Her birth certificate or baptism, her 1976 marriage license to Gilbert Zaloom (only an index line), and her death certificate of January 2000 are not yet in hand. A family member can ask for the death certificate. For Gilbert we would also like his 1940 census page, draft card, army enlistment and Saint John burial record (1995), and the Caravan of 21 May 1959, which an index says reported his marriage to a Helen Batrouney.

  • The five children. We have no birth or baptism records for them, and only one marriage license (an index line). A 1951 Arabic paper calls a baby girl “the third child” when four had been born: a miscount, or “third daughter”? A question for the family.

  • Awn’s Specialty Shop and Koury-Awn, Inc. The first advertisement we found is 29 October 1946 and the last 15 June 1984; we do not know when the shop opened or closed. We would like to know who ran Koury-Awn, Inc. (Anthony’s employer in 1940, at 38 East 32nd Street). 82 Greenwich Street was John’s business address on his draft cards before the shop ads; who ran that business is not known. Photographs of the shop would help.

  • Joseph and Assin in Lebanon. We have found no church or civil records from Damour. Parish and civil registers there could take us back past Joseph.

  • Joseph’s grave and age. His 1932 certificate prints a birth date of 21 November 1869, but its age of 62 years, 9 months and 7 days fits about 7 December 1869. Find a Grave and the cemetery’s own search give different grave numbers for him in Section 3, Row C. Saint John’s full records, including why Joseph, Assin and George were moved in June 1961, have not been requested.

  • Michal Malleut. The 1920 passenger list names “Uncle, Michal Malleut, Damour”, and Nassib’s border card names him as nearest relative. Neither says who he was. A question for the family.

  • The children’s ages. The 1925 census ages for six of the nine children (Aseen, George, Michael, Elias, Matilda and Anthony) do not match the 1920 passenger list plus five years. Matilda’s age is given as 29 in 1920, 23 in 1925, and by her petition’s birth date of 16 October 1899 would be 20 or 21 in 1920. The Eagle of 5 December 1940 also says John came with four brothers, where the list shows five (Nassib’s death in 1924 may explain it, but this is a guess). A question for the family.

  • John’s and Theresa’s birth dates. John’s papers give 15 August 1905, 15 August 1904, 4 August 1903 and 15 August 1903. The court’s decision on his birth date has not been found; it would be in the Kings County court or naturalization file for his petition. His earlier declaration of 1926 and Theresa’s second alien registration card have not been asked for.

  • Elias’s arrival. His papers give his entry at Rouses Point as 7 September 1920; the others’ papers give 5 September. The family says he was held back by an eye infection. No record in hand shows an infection.

  • The Baltic Street household. In 1920, Sammel (Selim) Owen’s household was at 202 Baltic Street. The census page reads his wife’s first name as Hedda or Fedda; the family’s email says Freda. We would like to know how the family was related to them, and the Quebec sheet names only “Mrs. Owen”.

  • Fred Gorayeb and Matilda. Fred’s birth is given as 17 December 1889, 2 October 1889, 5 November 1889 and 17 October 1890, and his arrival as 1902 and 1903. We have found nothing on his death, or where he and Matilda are buried. An index line says Matilda probably died in December 1984.

  • When and where Theresa died. The obituary indexes point to newspaper clippings we have not read.

  • Elias, Isabel and Hicks Street. Isabel’s 1937 marriage certificate prints her parents as Fred and Sarah Ganimy; Paterson, New Jersey records may say more about them.

  • Anthony’s school years. A 1932 Hamilton High School class list or yearbook would show whether the “Anthony Aoun” in the Eagle is our Anthony. Brooklyn city directories for 37 Tompkins Place, 1925 to 1932, have not been searched.

  • George Awn. He drowned on Sunday 27 July 1930. The Arabic papers disagree on where: Long Island (a canoe on a lake, or the shore), a lake in Rhode Island, or a lake about fifty miles from Brooklyn. They also differ on when he left home (Saturday, or Sunday morning after Mass), the hour of the service (two or three in the afternoon), and who the friend was. His age is given as 20 (1920 list), 23 (1925 census), 22 and about 24 (the notices). A New York death record, the New York papers of 28 and 29 July 1930, or a native Arabic reader for the notices would help.

  • Nassib’s death. His certificate and three paid notices say Thursday evening, 2 October 1924; one Arabic paper says “last Friday”. Which brother had the arm surgery in the family story is not known (Nassib’s certificate names pneumonia and tuberculosis).

  • Mitchell’s marriages. Mitchell married his first wife on 14 October 1940 (Yonkers, by his petition) and later Laurdes Nassr (a family tree says 1947). We do not know when the second marriage took place or how the first ended. His 1947 deed has not been read.

  • Paul Awn’s father. A 1976 column says “whose father Louis”; Find a Grave gives Mitchell J. Awn. There may be two men named Paul Awn. He is not a child of Anthony and Helen.

  • Joseph A. Awn’s death date. The family’s memorial card says 18 January 1989; Find a Grave gives 20 January. The cemetery’s burial record and a January 1989 death notice would settle it.

  • Esther Ayoub’s family in Beirut. The 1961 Caravan notice names her son and two daughters in Beirut. It names no husband; Arabic papers of 1930 name As’ad Aoun as Esther’s husband (our translation). We do not have her birth, marriage or the date of her death in January 1961. Does anyone remember them?

The Saydah family

  • Antoon’s birth year. His own papers say 15 August 1881; the 1900 census and his ship’s age of 18 fit 1880; one 1915 age points to 1878 or 1879. A Forzul baptism record would settle it, but we have found none.

  • Antoon’s ship and arrival. He swore he arrived on La Touraine on 25 October 1898. The only matching entry, “Antoun Smyda,” 18, is on a list dated 28 November 1898 and may not be him. Several records give about 1900 or 1901 instead, though the 1900 census and his 1923 passport application say 1898. We need the 25 October sailing’s page, or a record that rules the Smyda entry in or out.

  • 73 Washington Street, 1898. The La Touraine entry says he was joining a brother at 73 Washington Street. That is probably Khaleel, but we have not shown where Khaleel lived in 1898. A grocer named John Haddad is listed at 73 Washington Street in an 1898 Brooklyn directory, which looks like a coincidence of number only.

  • Antoon’s naturalization. His petition shows a certificate number of 381478, issued 22 January 1914, with the last digit written over; a handwritten number beside his name on the 1924 Majestic list is different. The certificate itself has not been seen.

  • Wassela’s birth year and birthplace. The 1900 census says May 1886; later records fit about 1889 to 1893 (the Social Security index says 20 December 1889). Is Zahleh really her birthplace? Her 1969 death certificate may give a date; we do not have it, only a newspaper index line (The Record of Hackensack, 8 January 1969) and a sympathy notice. Her death is given as December 1968 by one index and 6 January 1969 by two others; she was buried on 9 January 1969.

  • The church. Anthony’s side was Maronite, at Our Lady of Lebanon. One Arabic paper of January 1941 says the bride was not Maronite, and the Saydahs used Presbyterian and Episcopal churches (Khaleel studied at a Presbyterian seminary, and Antoon’s funeral was at Christ Episcopal). The December 1940 registers of Our Lady of Lebanon and St. Anselm’s, and Holy Trinity Church’s 1912 register, may say more.

  • The end of Antoon’s life. His South Carolina death certificate and the Eagle name Allendale; two Arabic papers say Columbia. It is probably Allendale; a clearer copy of the certificate would settle it. The church for his funeral is Christ Episcopal in two papers and a street near “76th Street and Fourth Avenue” in the third. His burial is given as Monday 21 November 1949 by one paper and 11 February 1950 by Green-Wood (perhaps a vault). We have not asked Green-Wood for the full records for Antoon and Wassela, and we have no will or estate file.

  • The Tuxedo Park accident, 1916. The papers disagree on whose car it was, who was driving (Antoon or Khaleel) and whether the others were hurt. We do not know how the new trial ordered in December 1918 ended (the last mention is a January 1919 court calendar), and we have not found Najib Maloof’s death certificate (his age at death is 50, 51 or 52 depending on the paper), the court file, or the names of the other men in the car.

  • Antoon’s brothers. Khaleel and Michael say they arrived in 1891, before Ellis Island opened, so the Ellis Island database will not have them. No village register for Forzul is in any collection we use, and the Ottoman population registers have not been searched for the family.

  • Khaleel’s birth. Records give 19 March 1870 (his death certificate), March 1868, about 1867, about 1870, 15 March 1866 and 19 March 1867 (his passport applications). His Beirut degree is dated 1887 by one paper and 1888 by two other sources.

  • Khaleel’s arrival. He says 1891; one 1915 age points to about 1903. The certificate’s 47 years points to about 1901 for New York, but he was at 73 Washington Street in 1900. His Cook County naturalization of 2 November 1896 is known from his own statements and an index card; the petition and certificate have not been seen. We have not found a will or estate file, or Green-Wood’s full records (he appears twice, 29 November 1948 and 1 October 1949).

  • Khaleel’s wife and son. We know Katherine’s family name only as Kourani or Kourany (probably). Her age is 40 on the 1915 census but about 32 on three other records (1882 to 1883). Edward’s birth certificate (Brooklyn, about August 1906) has not been ordered.

  • Michael’s birth and arrival. His own records say 6 August 1877; his sons’ certificates imply about 1869 to 1870. He says he sailed in October 1891, but the 1925 census gives about 1889 for his years in the U.S. We cannot show when he moved from Chicago to Brooklyn: he was there by September 1918 (his draft card).

  • Michael’s naturalization. His own statements say Cook County, 18 March 1903; the 1925 census says Brooklyn, 1915. We have not seen the certificate.

  • Michael’s marriages. We do not know what became of his first wife, Marie Luckey (Grundy County, Illinois, 1901; only an index line is in hand). The Arabic press puts his marriage to Zakeya in Chicago on 5 February 1909, but we have no record of it. Her age is 28 or 32 in 1909 and 1913 (about 1881) and 34 in 1925 (about 1891); her death record (Green-Wood burial, 26 February 1966) should settle it.

  • Michael’s children. His first son’s first name is Jameel on the Cook County birth entry and the 1925 census and James on the 1916 consular certificate. Which did he use? We also lack cemetery records for his infant sons Victor (1919) and Theodore (1922; Mount Olivet), and a death record for his infant daughter Samura (1912), known from an index line.

  • Michael in 1953. His 1953 notices give 8646 Fort Hamilton Parkway, probably a later address than the 8726 of 1922 to 1925. We do not have his death certificate (1 March 1953) or Green-Wood’s full record. Who one bridesmaid at a 1951 wedding was is a question for the family.

  • Murad Saydah. We have no record of when or where Murad died, only that he was alive in January 1920 and his widow Tirkman was widowed by 1929. The 1920 census puts a Murad Saydah, 48, at a jewelry store in Oak Park, and also a Murad Sayyidah, 48, at the Chicago State Hospital four days later; whether they are one man is open. His naturalization is an index card of 19 March 1895 against “about 1897” in his son Harold’s papers.

  • Arrival years for Murad and Tirkman. Murad’s arrival is about February 1890 in one paper, 1886 as we read the 1920 census, and 1891 in his son Ferris’s 1919 registration. Tirkman’s years in the U.S. are 35 in 1925 and 37 in 1929.

  • Tirkman. Her family name is written many ways (Bouinain, Bouman, Abu Aynin and others). Her age at death is 61 or 51 (48 on the 1925 census), and the day of her funeral is Tuesday 10 September 1929 in the Arabic notice and “to-morrow” in the Brooklyn Daily Times of that day. We do not know when she returned from Syria in 1927 or 1928, or who signed Nora’s 1920 papers.

  • Ferris and Fares. Ferris’s birth day is 2 or 26 February 1896, his 1925 census age is a year off both, and his death is placed at Presbyterian Hospital, Manhattan, on the certificate and in Tenafly in an Arabic notice (the certificate is probably right). The wedding of Fares and Alice is 18 December 1926 on the certificate and 11 December in the Arabic notice. His sworn 1919 application says 2 February 1896, so that is probably right; his 1921 and 1923 passport applications may confirm it.

  • Sada and Nustas Kirkish. The 1948, 1949 and 1953 notices name them as Antoon’s sisters. Sada is probably “Sady,” wife of Abdalla Kirkish, in whose Michigan household Antoon boarded in 1900. We have not found a record naming their parents. Her husband is called Abdalo, Assad, Abdalla and Abdullah in different records (probably two men); Nustas’s is “Marun,” Michael and Mike. The 1953 notice says only “California” for both sisters. Sada’s Michigan death record, and the Kirkish naturalization and death records for Marquette County, might settle these.

  • Sada’s and Nustas’s birth years. Sada’s birth is 1874 or 1875 in the census and the California death index, but 14 May 1880 in a Social Security line that may be a different Sadie. Nustas’s is 17 June 1884 in the Social Security index and about 1885 to 1887 in other records.

  • Mary Abraham. In 1920 she is the Kirkish head’s mother-in-law, but Antoon’s mother was Noor. She may be misrecorded, a step-relation, or the Sadie in that household may be someone else. Her own age differs by five years between 1900 and 1920. A question for the family.

  • The Kirkish children. Naseem’s birth is 1899 in Michigan (a birth return of 14 October 1899; the 1900 census says December); a 1922 marriage record that says “born Syria” is probably a different Naseeb. Richard’s middle name is Elias on his birth certificate and John on his death certificate (one boy, probably). The 1920 Forsyth census page reads “Sada” for the wife of a Mikel Kirkish, but the sons are Nustas’s, so she is probably Nustas under a wrong first name. Sada’s immigration year is 1891 in 1900 and 1897 in the 1920 Munising census; a 1912 entry is probably a different Sadie.

  • Evelyn, Virginia and Eli. The 1940 census sheet we found lists only Antoon, Wassela and Helen. Virginia’s marriage to George Hakim is known from newspapers and indexes (on 6 May 1945, though one paper’s “next Sunday” would give 29 April) and Evelyn’s marriages (1953 and 1971 licenses) from indexes. None of the certificates has been ordered, and we do not know where Eli Zrake is buried or who his first wife was beyond the Arabic press of October 1945. Eli’s age at death is 38 or 39.

  • Virginia’s and Evelyn’s births. Virginia’s birth is 29 June 1921 in her index line and about December 1921 by the 1930 census. Evelyn’s 1920 index line spells the family name “Saydak”. Their birth certificates have not been ordered, and we have not shown that “Evelyn Z. Deschner” in Green-Wood’s index is Evelyn Saydah.

  • The Zrake family. Matilda Zrake’s birth is 20 January 1901, 20 November 1901 or 1900, and her first initial is N in one 1962 notice. Green-Wood lists a Matilda buried in December 1954, but several later prints treat her as living. We do not know when Najeeb Zrake died, between 1944 and October 1945, or whether he made a 1914 declaration of intention in Norwich, Connecticut.

  • George Hakim. A 1956 Caravan calls George Hakim the brother of “Mrs. Anthony Awn”; our records make him Virginia’s husband, so Helen’s brother-in-law. No record explains it. A native reader should also check the Arabic engagement, wedding and birth notices for the Hakim and Zuraiq families.

The Hadad (Hodod) and Shehadi families

  • The 1892 arrival. We have not found the arrival record for John (Hanna) Hadad, Amina and the children. The censuses give 1892 for the Hadad family; Wassela’s own entries give 1893 and 1895.

  • Hanna Hadad’s birth, death and burial. His birth year is about 1845 on three records and 1850 on the 1900 census. Syracuse papers and an Arabic notice say he died at Clifton Springs; the state death index says Willard (certificate 50442). One paper says he was buried at Morningside Cemetery and Find a Grave says Oakwood Cemetery, Syracuse, with no marker. One paper says eighteen months at Clifton Springs, another eight. His death certificate and the cemetery records would settle these.

  • The Shehadis. Were they Hanna’s brothers-in-law or cousins? Two papers say brothers-in-law and one says cousins (probably brothers-in-law). One Arabic notice names a son-in-law Antoon; another names “Amin Sayda” as the daughter’s husband. Joseph J. Shehadi’s arrival is given as 1892, 1894 or 1897, and his wife Nabeha’s as 1906 or 1908. Frederick Shehadi’s age (13 months in 1907, about five in 1909) cannot be squared yet.

  • Wassela’s brothers and other Haddad men. Salim Hadad’s age is 26 on a 1913 notice but 28 or 29 by the 1900 census; his World War I draft card has not been found. A Najib Haddad buried in Syracuse (1895 to 1955) has two different birth dates, neither matching the 1900 census (October 1896); his Oakwood burial record and Watertown death certificate may show whether he is ours. We do not know where Charles Haddad was in 1900, or what became of Beshara Shahady’s family. Who the Najeeb Hadad of 4721 Sixth Avenue (died 13 January 1914) was is also open.

  • The Hadads’ 1897 arrival sheet. It reads “98 Washington Street”; the Ellis Island index card reads “89” (faint).

  • Helen Hadad and Lily. Helen Hadad’s death date is 21 April 1908 on her certificate and 12 April in an Arabic paper. Mount Olivet’s lot records may show whether she and Lily (1913) share a lot, and who bought it.

  • People at the 1912 wedding. Who “R. G. Haddad” (the physician on Lily’s certificate) and Maheeba Fuleihan (a witness) were, and whether they were relatives, is a question for the family.

The Merhige family

  • Amin and Helen Merhige. Amin’s landing is about 1893 on his declaration, about 1901 on a 1941 index and 1900 on the 1930 census. Helen’s years in the U.S. are 1895, 1900 and 1913 in different records, though she is already in the 1905 and 1910 censuses. Georgette’s birth (10 January 1910 by her petition) clashes with her census ages and with when she says she married. We have no burial place for Amin, no death certificates for Amin, Habib or Georgette, and no burial place for the infants.

  • The Merhige connection. A 1897 sheet shows two Hadads going to a “cousin Amin” at the address where Amin Merhige’s brothers were headed. It does not settle whether this Wassilah is Wassela. A question for the family.

  • The 11th Avenue house. The family says Amin built it. Brooklyn building permits and the Kings County deed index for 8314 11th Avenue should show the builder and the year.

Everyone

  • Records we cannot yet read. Several Arabic pages are our own translations. A native Arabic reader should check them, especially the notices for George, the Hakim and Zuraiq families, and Tirkman’s.

  • Living memories. A conversation with family would fill in what no record keeps.
